Start with a 12 × 12 inch sheet of cardboard. Cut a square of side x from each corner and fold up the sides to make an open box. Cut too little and the box is flat. Cut too much and it’s a narrow tower. What cut gives the most volume? Search with the slider, and watch where the point sits on the volume graph when you find it.

The calculus way

\( V(x) = x(12 - 2x)^2 \) on \( 0 \lt x \lt 6 \). The best x is where the graph peaks, which is where \( V'(x) = 0 \). No slider needed: \( V'(x) = (12 - 2x)(12 - 6x) = 0 \) gives \( x = 2 \) (x = 6 is outside the domain).

  1. Understand and draw. What’s being maximized or minimized? What’s fixed?
  2. Name variables and write the objective, the quantity to optimize, like \( A = xy \).
  3. Write the constraint, the equation linking the variables, like \( 2x + y = 2400 \).
  4. Use the constraint to make the objective a function of one variable.
  5. Find the domain that makes physical sense (lengths ≥ 0, and so on).
  6. Calculus: critical numbers, then compare with the endpoints (Closed Interval Method), or use the First/Second Derivative Test.
  7. Answer the actual question, with units. Does it ask for the dimensions, or for the maximum area?
Common mistakes

Differentiating before reducing to one variable. Forgetting to justify that a critical number is actually a max or min. Answering with x when the question asks for the area. Minimizing distance directly when minimizing distance² is much easier (it’s the same point).

Exact answers or decimals to three places are both accepted.
