Up to now, x approached a number. Now let x run off toward \( \infty \) (and \( -\infty \)). Zoom way out on each function. Which ones settle toward a horizontal line, and which ones don’t? Before you zoom, look at the highest powers of x on top and bottom.

\( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = L \) means \( f(x) \) gets as close to L as we like once x is large enough. Then \( y = L \) is a horizontal asymptote. The same goes for \( x \to -\infty \). A graph can have a different asymptote on each end, or none.

The basic building block: for any \( r \gt 0 \),

\[ \lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{1}{x^r} = 0. \]

Dividing a fixed number by a huge number gives something tiny.

Rational functions: divide by the highest power in the denominator

Degree of top vs. bottom\( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} \)Example

The table is a shortcut. On exams you’re usually expected to show the division.

Roots and negative infinity

The \( \sqrt{x^2} \) trap

\( \sqrt{x^2} = |x| \), not x. When \( x \to -\infty \), x is negative, so \( \sqrt{x^2} = -x \). Forgetting this flips the sign of your answer.

∞ − ∞ is indeterminate

\( \sqrt{x^2 + x} - x \) looks like “∞ − ∞,” which, like 0/0, could be anything. Multiply by the conjugate to turn the difference into a quotient, then divide by the highest power.

Other functions to know

  • \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} e^{-x} = 0 \) and \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} e^{x} = 0 \), so \( y = 0 \) is a horizontal asymptote of \( e^x \) on the left.
  • \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} \arctan x = \tfrac{\pi}{2} \) and \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} \arctan x = -\tfrac{\pi}{2} \), which gives two different asymptotes.
  • \( \sin x \) and \( \cos x \) oscillate forever, so they have no limit at \( \infty \). But \( \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 0 \) by the Squeeze Theorem.
