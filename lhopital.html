A \( \frac00 \) limit means both the top function f and the bottom function g pass through zero at the same point. Zoom in there. Both curves turn into straight lines (Topic 2.1), and the ratio of two lines through the same point is just the ratio of their slopes.

■ top, f   ■ bottom, g

The insight

Near a common zero, \( f(x) \approx f'(a)(x - a) \) and \( g(x) \approx g'(a)(x - a) \). The \( (x - a) \) cancels, so \( \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} \approx \dfrac{f'(a)}{g'(a)} \). That’s L’Hôpital’s Rule.

L’Hôpital’s Rule

If \( \displaystyle\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \) has the form \( \frac00 \) or \( \frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty} \), then

\[ \lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} \]

provided the right-hand limit exists (or is ±∞). It works for \( x \to a \), one-sided limits, and \( x \to \pm\infty \).

Three rules about the rule

1. Check the form first. On anything other than 0/0 or ∞/∞ it gives wrong answers. 2. Differentiate the top and bottom separately. It is not the quotient rule. 3. After each application, check the form again. You may need to apply it twice, or not at all.

Growth rates

Repeated L’Hôpital shows \( \dfrac{x^n}{e^x} \to 0 \) and \( \dfrac{\ln x}{x^p} \to 0 \) as \( x \to \infty \). Exponentials beat powers, and powers beat logs. That ranking settles most limits at infinity at a glance.
