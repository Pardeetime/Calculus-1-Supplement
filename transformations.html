What to notice

Changes outside the function (a and k) act on the outputs, so they move the graph vertically the way you’d expect. Changes inside (b and h) act on the inputs, so they move it horizontally, and they feel backwards.

Take \( y = (x-3)^2 \). The parent \( x^2 \) has its lowest point when the input is 0. The new function feeds \( x - 3 \) into the square, so it hits that same low point when \( x - 3 = 0 \), which means \( x = 3 \). Everything happens 3 units later, so the graph moves right, even though the formula shows a minus.

Common mistake

In \( y = (2x - 6)^2 \) the shift is not 6. First factor the inside: \( (2(x - 3))^2 \). Now you can read it: squeeze by \( \tfrac12 \), then shift right 3. Always factor out b before reading h.

Combining functions

You can add, subtract, multiply and divide functions output by output: \( (f+g)(x) = f(x) + g(x) \), \( (fg)(x) = f(x)\,g(x) \), and so on. The new domain is where both \( f \) and \( g \) are defined. For \( f/g \), you also remove any x where \( g(x) = 0 \).

Composition: one function inside another

\( (f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x)) \) means: do g first, then feed its output into f. It’s an assembly line. Try switching the order of the two machines:

\( g(x) = x + 3 \) (add 3), and \( f(x) = x^2 \) (square).

Why calculus cares

The Chain Rule (Unit 3) is how you differentiate a composition. It depends on seeing the inside and outside functions. In \( \sqrt{x^2 + 5} \), the inside is \( x^2 + 5 \) and the outside is the square root. Practice spotting them now.
