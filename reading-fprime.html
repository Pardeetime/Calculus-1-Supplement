Hilltops and valleys

Where \( f' \) changes sign, f turns around:

  • \( f' \) goes from + to −: f rises, then falls, giving a local maximum.
  • \( f' \) goes from − to +: f falls, then rises, giving a local minimum.
  • \( f' = 0 \) but no sign change (like \( x^3 \) at 0): a flat spot, but no turn.

Unit 4 makes this the First Derivative Test.

Going backwards: from f′ to f

A graph of \( f' \) doesn’t tell you the height of f, only its shape. Any vertical shift of f has the same derivative. So from \( f' \), you describe f with a sign chart.

A look ahead

If \( f' \) is increasing, the slopes of f are getting larger, so f bends upward (concave up). That’s \( f'' \gt 0 \). Unit 4 builds curve sketching on exactly this chain: f, then f′, then f″.
