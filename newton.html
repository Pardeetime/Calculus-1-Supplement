To solve \( f(x) = 0 \), start with a guess \( x_0 \). Replace the curve with its tangent line there. A line is easy to solve, so find where the tangent hits zero and use that as your next guess. Repeat. Press Step and watch the guesses close in. Then try the last two functions to see how the method can go wrong.

The tangent at \( x_n \) is \( y = f(x_n) + f'(x_n)(x - x_n) \). Set \( y = 0 \) and solve for x:

Newton’s Method
\[ x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} \]
Speed

Near a simple root, Newton’s method roughly doubles the number of correct digits with every step. That’s why calculators and computers use it (or cousins of it) to compute square roots and solve equations.

How it fails

Flat tangent: if \( f'(x_n) = 0 \), the tangent never crosses zero and the formula divides by zero. Cycling: for \( x^3 - 2x + 2 \) starting at 0, the guesses bounce 0 → 1 → 0 → 1 forever. Running away: for \( \sqrt[3]{x} \), each step lands twice as far from the root. A good starting guess (use a graph!) avoids most trouble.

Exact fractions or decimals to four places are fine.
