Radians

A radian measures an angle by the arc it cuts off on the unit circle. A full turn is the whole circumference, \( 2\pi \), so \( 360^\circ = 2\pi \) and \( 180^\circ = \pi \).

\[ \text{radians} = \text{degrees} \times \frac{\pi}{180}, \qquad \text{degrees} = \text{radians} \times \frac{180}{\pi} \]
Why calculus insists on radians

Only in radians is \( \dfrac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x \). That rests on the limit \( \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1 \) as \( x \to 0 \), which works because for small angles the arc (θ) and the height (sin θ) are almost the same length. In degrees, an extra factor of \( \pi/180 \) shows up everywhere.

Definitions from the circle

For the point where angle \( \theta \) meets the unit circle:

\[ \cos\theta = x, \qquad \sin\theta = y, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} \;\;(\text{the slope of the radius}) \]

The reciprocals are \( \sec\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} \), \( \csc\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta} \) and \( \cot\theta = \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \).

Any other quadrant: find the reference angle (the acute angle back to the x-axis), use the value from the table, then fix the sign by where the point is. Sine is positive above the x-axis (quadrants I and II), and cosine is positive to the right of the y-axis (quadrants I and IV).

Identities worth knowing

The point \( (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) \) is on a circle of radius 1, so the Pythagorean theorem gives the most important identity:

\[ \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \]

Divide it by \( \cos^2\theta \) to get \( \tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta \). A few others:

  • Even/odd: \( \cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta \) and \( \sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta \) (reflect the point across the x-axis).
  • Periodic: \( \sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta \), \( \cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta \), \( \tan(\theta + \pi) = \tan\theta \).
  • Double angle: \( \sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta \) and \( \cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta \).
