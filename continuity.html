Definition

\( f \) is continuous at a if \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a) \). That packs in three checks:

  1. \( f(a) \) is defined,
  2. \( \displaystyle\lim_{x\to a} f(x) \) exists, and
  3. they’re equal.
TypeWhat failsPicture
RemovableThe limit exists, but \( f(a) \) is missing or differentA hole, possibly with a stray dot
JumpThe one-sided limits exist but differ, so the limit doesn’t existA step
InfiniteA one-sided limit is \( \pm\infty \)A vertical asymptote

Removable means you could fix it by redefining one point. Jumps and infinite discontinuities can’t be fixed that way.

Where functions are continuous: polynomials everywhere; rational functions, roots, trig, exponential and log functions everywhere on their domains. Sums, products, quotients (where the denominator isn’t 0) and compositions of continuous functions are continuous. So in practice, look for trouble only at domain breaks and at the seams of piecewise functions.

A function is continuous on a closed interval \( [a, b] \) if it’s continuous inside and continuous from the right at a and from the left at b.

Fixing a seam

\( g(x) = \begin{cases} c\,x, & x \lt 2 \\ x^2 - c, & x \ge 2 \end{cases} \). The two pieces meet at \( x = 2 \) only for one value of c. Find it with the slider, then find it with algebra.

Algebra: continuity at 2 needs left limit = right limit = value: \( 2c = 4 - c \), so \( c = \tfrac43 \). Set the pieces equal at the seam and solve.

The Intermediate Value Theorem

IVT

If \( f \) is continuous on \( [a, b] \) and N is any number between \( f(a) \) and \( f(b) \), then there is at least one c in \( (a, b) \) with \( f(c) = N \).

Its biggest use is proving a root exists: if f is continuous and changes sign on \( [a, b] \), take \( N = 0 \). Keep halving the interval and you can trap the root as tightly as you want:

\( f(x) = x^3 + x - 1 \) on \( [0, 1] \): \( f(0) = -1 \lt 0 \) and \( f(1) = 1 \gt 0 \).
