Take \( y = \sin(x^2) \). It’s a two-stage machine: \( x \) goes into \( u = x^2 \), and then \( u \) goes into \( y = \sin u \). Nudge x by a small amount Δx. The first stage stretches that nudge into Δu, and the second stretches Δu into Δy. How does the total stretch relate to the two stage stretches?

The Chain Rule in one sentence

If u changes \( \frac{du}{dx} \) times as fast as x, and y changes \( \frac{dy}{du} \) times as fast as u, then y changes \( \frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx} \) times as fast as x. Like gears: the stretch factors multiply.

The Chain Rule
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\,f\big(g(x)\big) = f'\big(g(x)\big)\cdot g'(x) \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx} \]

Outside-inside method: differentiate the outside function, leaving the inside untouched. Then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

FormDerivative
\( [g(x)]^n \)\( n[g(x)]^{n-1}\cdot g'(x) \)
\( e^{g(x)} \)\( e^{g(x)}\cdot g'(x) \)
\( \sin g(x) \)\( \cos g(x)\cdot g'(x) \)
\( \sqrt{g(x)} \)\( \dfrac{g'(x)}{2\sqrt{g(x)}} \)
The two classic mistakes

1. Forgetting the inside derivative: \( \frac{d}{dx}\sin(x^2) \ne \cos(x^2) \). It needs the factor \( 2x \).
2. Changing the inside: \( \frac{d}{dx}\sin(x^2) \ne \cos(2x) \). The outside derivative keeps the original inside, \( x^2 \).

Chains of three or more

Peel one layer at a time, from outside to inside, multiplying as you go.

Why it matters

Almost every function you’ll meet from here on is a composition. The Chain Rule is behind implicit differentiation (3.6), log derivatives (3.7), inverse trig (3.8) and related rates (3.9). It’s worth getting completely comfortable with it.
