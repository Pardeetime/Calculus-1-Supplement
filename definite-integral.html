When a function dips below the x-axis, its Riemann-sum rectangles have negative heights. So the integral counts area above the axis as positive and area below as negative. Move the limits a and b. Can you make the total come out to exactly zero?

Definition
\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \]

It’s a number, the net signed area between f and the x-axis from a to b. The \( \int \) is a stretched S (for “sum”), and \( dx \) is what \( \Delta x \) becomes in the limit. Every continuous function on \( [a, b] \) is integrable.

PropertyWhy it makes sense
\( \int_a^a f = 0 \)A region with no width has no area
\( \int_b^a f = -\int_a^b f \)Going right to left makes \( \Delta x \) negative
\( \int_a^b c\,f = c\int_a^b f \)Stretching heights stretches the area
\( \int_a^b (f \pm g) = \int_a^b f \pm \int_a^b g \)Heights add, so areas add
\( \int_a^c f + \int_c^b f = \int_a^b f \)Areas of adjacent pieces add
\( \int_a^b c\,dx = c(b - a) \)The area of a rectangle
If \( f \le g \) then \( \int_a^b f \le \int_a^b g \)A lower curve has less area

Evaluating with geometry

Until the Fundamental Theorem (next lesson), you can evaluate integrals of lines and circles by recognizing the shapes.

Symmetry shortcut

If f is odd, then \( \int_{-a}^{a} f = 0 \) (the areas cancel). If f is even, then \( \int_{-a}^{a} f = 2\int_0^a f \).
