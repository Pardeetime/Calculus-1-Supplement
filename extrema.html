Here’s \( f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1 \), looked at only on an interval \( [a, b] \) that you choose. The highest and lowest points of that piece are marked. Move the endpoints around. Where do the extremes show up? Only in a few kinds of places.

  1. Can you make the highest point land at an endpoint? At an interior point?
  2. When an extreme is inside the interval, what does the tangent line look like there?
What you found

Absolute extremes on a closed interval happen at only two kinds of places: the endpoints, or interior points where the tangent is flat (or where there’s a corner). That short list is what makes them findable.

TermMeaning
Absolute max at c\( f(c) \ge f(x) \) for every x in the domain, the highest point overall
Local max at c\( f(c) \ge f(x) \) for x near c, a hilltop
Critical numberA c in the domain with \( f'(c) = 0 \) or \( f'(c) \) undefined
Extreme Value Theorem

If f is continuous on a closed interval \( [a, b] \), then f has an absolute max and an absolute min there. Remove either condition and it can fail: \( \tfrac1x \) on \( (0, 1] \) has no max, and a function with a jump can skip over its would-be peak.

Fermat’s Theorem

If f has a local max or min at an interior point c, then c is a critical number. The converse is false: \( x^3 \) has \( f'(0) = 0 \) with no max or min there. Critical numbers are candidates, not guarantees.

The Closed Interval Method

  1. Find the critical numbers of f inside \( (a, b) \).
  2. Evaluate f at each of them and at both endpoints.
  3. The largest value is the absolute max, and the smallest is the absolute min.
Common mistakes

Forgetting to check the endpoints. Including critical numbers that lie outside the interval. Missing points where \( f' \) is undefined, like the corner of \( |x| \) or the cusp of \( x^{2/3} \). And answering with the x-value when the question asks for the max value (a y-value), or the other way around.
