A 10-ft ladder leans against a wall. Someone pulls the bottom away from the wall at a steady 1 ft/s. Predict first: does the top slide down the wall at a steady speed too? Then press play.

Watch the top’s speed near the end.

What happened

The bottom moves at a constant rate, but the top speeds up, faster and faster as it nears the floor. The two rates are related through the equation \( x^2 + y^2 = 100 \), and that relationship changes as the ladder falls. That’s a related-rates problem.

Every quantity is a function of time t. Differentiating an equation that links the quantities, with respect to t, links their rates. It’s implicit differentiation (Topic 3.6) with t playing the role of x.

  1. Draw a picture and label the quantities that change with variables.
  2. List the rate you know (with units and sign) and the rate you want.
  3. Write an equation relating the variables. Geometry is usually the source: Pythagoras, area, volume, similar triangles.
  4. Differentiate both sides with respect to t. Every variable gets a \( \frac{d}{dt} \).
  5. Now substitute the numbers for the moment in question, and solve.
  6. Answer in a sentence with units. A negative rate means the quantity is decreasing.
The #1 mistake

Substituting a changing value before differentiating. If you plug in \( x = 6 \) first, x becomes a constant, its rate becomes 0, and the problem falls apart. Only constants, like the ladder’s length 10, go in before step 4.

Exact answers (with π, fractions) or decimals to three places are both accepted.
