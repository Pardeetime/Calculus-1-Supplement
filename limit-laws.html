What you saw

Four limits gave the same “answer” on substitution, 0/0, but four different real answers: 4, ¼, 0, and no limit at all. 0/0 is not an answer. It tells you the substitution was hiding something, and the algebra in this lesson removes the disguise.

The limit laws

If \( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) \) and \( \lim_{x\to a} g(x) \) both exist, then limits split across arithmetic:

LawStatement
Sum / difference\( \lim (f \pm g) = \lim f \pm \lim g \)
Constant multiple\( \lim c\,f = c \lim f \)
Product\( \lim (f g) = (\lim f)(\lim g) \)
Quotient\( \lim \dfrac{f}{g} = \dfrac{\lim f}{\lim g} \), only if \( \lim g \ne 0 \)
Power / root\( \lim f^n = (\lim f)^n \), \( \lim \sqrt[n]{f} = \sqrt[n]{\lim f} \)

Together they give the direct substitution property: if \( f \) is a polynomial, a rational function, a root, a trig, exponential or log function (or a combination), and \( a \) is in its domain, then \( \lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a) \). Always try substitution first.

The decision flow

  1. SubstitutePlug in \( x = a \). What do you get?
  2. A numberThat’s the limit. You’re done.
  3. \( \dfrac{\text{nonzero}}{0} \)Infinite limit or DNE. Use sign analysis on each side (Topic 1.3).
  4. \( \dfrac{0}{0} \)Indeterminate, so do algebra: factor and cancel; multiply by the conjugate if there’s a root; get a common denominator if there are fractions inside fractions; expand if there are powers. Then substitute again.
Why canceling is legal

\( \dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2} \) and \( x + 2 \) are equal for every x except 2. A limit never looks at \( x = 2 \) itself, so the two have the same limit. Canceling doesn’t change the function anywhere the limit cares about.

Common mistake

Writing “\( = \frac00 \), so the limit doesn’t exist.” As you saw above, 0/0 can hide any answer. Also, keep writing “\( \lim_{x\to a} \)” in front of every line until the moment you substitute, since instructors take points off for dropping it.

Substitute first every time. Then choose your tool.
